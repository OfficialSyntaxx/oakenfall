Chapter II — The Trades

Gatherers

Woodcutters, miners, foragers, hunters, and fishers keep raw goods flowing. Every gatherer works a real spot on the map and hauls the load home by hand.

Crafters

Sawyers, bakers, and other processors turn raw goods into planks, bread, and better things — if their building stands, has supply, and hasn't decayed.

Farmers

Fields follow the seasons: sow in spring, tend through summer, race the frost at harvest. A full granary is the difference between a hard winter and a funeral.

Guards

Knights and watchmen man the towers and palisades. Bandit raids probe your weakest wall — an unguarded hold is an invitation.

Chapter III — The River & The Roads

Bridges & fords

The river splits every hold. Two shallow rows let settlers wade across slowly with no bridge; build a bridge and they cross freely, opening the far bank's timber, stone, and hunting grounds.

The river as a moat

In defense the river counts as a natural moat — but every bridge erodes that bonus, so each crossing is a real trade-off. A guard post within three tiles keeps a bridge "watched" so it no longer weakens your walls, and raiders will name the unwatched span they slipped across.

Winter freeze

When winter bites the river freezes solid: anything can walk the ice — the moat bonus vanishes — and fishing halts until the thaw. Plan your defenses for the season the water turns to road.

Road logistics

Processors sitting beside a road network connected to the Town Center run about 12% faster. Paving isn't just for looks — a well-linked hold simply produces more.

Chapter IV — The Great Oak (Research)

Many branches

Research grows like the ancient oak the hold is named for: thirteen technologies and policies across two tiers, from sharpened tools to aqueducts and cold storage, each unlocking new buildings, bonuses, or ways to endure.

Hold tiers

As your settlement grows it rises through hold tiers — camp to village to true hold — opening the shop, daily bounties, and grander construction.

Chapter V — The Four Roads (Game Modes)

Settler

The gentle road. Forgiving pace, softer winters — learn the hold without burying half of it.

Peaceful

No raids, no wolves at the door. Just you, the land, and the seasons.

Iron Winter

The merciless road. Scarcer food, crueler cold, bolder bandits. For stewards with scars.

Merchant

Coin is king — trade-focused play built around the market, bounties, and the shop.

Chapter VI — Surviving Your First Winter

1. Wood before glory

Your first autumn is for firewood and food, not monuments. A cold villager is an unhappy one; a hungry one starts packing.

2. Repair before it rots

Buildings decay. Below 35 condition their bonuses stop entirely — patch roofs in autumn, not in a blizzard.

3. Watch morale

Morale is a slow tide. Taverns, full bellies, families, and safety raise it; illness, hunger, and raids drain it. Villagers who lose heart leave for good.

4. The hold works while you sleep

Close the game and your villagers keep at it at reduced pace. Come back to stocked shelves — within reason.

Chapter VII — Hardships of the Hold

Fire

Timber buildings can catch — worst in a dry summer. Flames spread between neighbours, so space your timber, pave stone breaks, and dig a Well nearby; tap a blaze to fling water, and settlers rush to help.

Blight

A sickness that strikes a crowd and spreads between those standing close. Herbal Lore softens it and a Healer clears the sick — keep folk from bunching when the fever comes.

Climate spells

Beyond the daily weather, droughts, cold snaps, and fair spells settle in for days at a time — cutting crops and raising fire risk, draining food and warmth, or blessing the fields. Watch the clock.

Livestock & guilds

A Pasture grazes a herd for hands-off food (but needs winter fodder), and two Masters of a trade found a guild that lifts that craft hold-wide. The deeper your hold, the more it rewards.

Chapter VIII — The Steward's Word

Most of what a steward wants is one sentence long, and the 🗣️ button takes it that way — plain words, no menu-diving.

Say what you want

"Build a farm and put two to mining." "We need more wood." "Mend the hold." Orders can be joined together, and the steward answers in kind — telling you what it did, or why it can't. Ask "how much stone" and it simply tells you.

It finds the hands

Told to staff a trade with everyone already busy, the steward pulls from whichever trade is most over-staffed rather than shrugging — but never strips one to nobody. Told to build, it picks a legal spot near the hold and sets it in motion.

What it won't do

It will not gather what you have nowhere to work: ask for stone with no Mining Post and it says so, rather than quietly assigning miners to stand in a field.

Chapter IX — Rule and Trade

Decrees

Four standing laws, each a real trade-off, toggled freely from the Goals tab. Curfew cuts the risk of a night raid by 30% but wears on spirits. Tithe fills the coffers daily at the cost of goodwill. Open Gates draws newcomers faster and makes the hold easier to reach. Rationing makes food last 20% longer and slows work by 6%. Change your reign whenever the season turns against you.

Trade routes

With an active Trading Post you can hold up to three caravan contracts: deliver a set amount of a good every two to four days for a steady flow of coin. Miss a delivery once and you get a warning; miss it twice and the route breaks — and a broken word costs the whole hold morale, not just the coin.

Coin and the shop

Coins come from daily bounties, completed goals, earned deeds and your caravans. They buy a feast, a summoned merchant, a healer's visit, repairs across the whole hold, emergency rations, or a new dye for the banner. The Trader's Ledger shows exactly where every coin came from and went.

The year's festival

Once a year the folk gather and you choose a blessing that holds until the next: Harvest Feast (+20% from fields and foragers), Rite of Courage (spirits stay higher, and a raid stings half as much), or Craftsmen's Fair (every settler works 15% faster).

Chapter X — The Lives of the Folk

They learn their trade

Time spent working is remembered. A settler becomes ✦ Skilled and then ★ Master at their craft, working faster for it, and a novice working within a few tiles of a Master of the same trade learns twice as fast. Two Masters of one trade found a guild that lifts that craft across the whole hold.

They make lives

Settlers form friendships and rivalries by working near one another, court, marry beneath the pines, and have children who grow up and take up the work. Every one of those moments is written into the Chronicle as it happens.

They grow old

Children are too young to work and play near home. Adults carry the hold. Elders slow down, and in time pass peacefully and are laid to rest in the memorial grove — where they stay, remembered, for as long as the hold stands.

Their own minds

Nobody is assigned a job by the game. An unemployed settler weighs what the hold actually needs and takes it up; an employed one reconsiders when the balance shifts, and one whose trade has run dry finds other work rather than standing idle.

Chapter XI — Your Own Story

Set a goal, or none

Play endlessly, or take an end-goal at founding: endure five winters, rise from outpost to town, saw three hundred planks, or turn back eight raids. Reaching one is a moment, not a wall — the notice congratulates you and gets out of the way. The hold is never taken from you.

Deeds

Fifteen one-time deeds mark what your hold has done — a first roof raised, a first winter survived, a bridge spanned, a first wedding, a settler who lived to be an elder. Several cannot be aimed for at all; they simply happen, and are remembered.

The Chronicle

Your hold keeps its own history in its own words — foundings, friendships, weddings, births, guilds formed, fires weathered, rulings made. It reads like something written rather than a log, and you can read the whole thing back at any time, or keep a chronicle card of it as a picture.

Paint your own land

The land editor lets you draw the ground yourself — grass, forest, stone, water, wilds — set where the hold begins, and play it. A finished land packs into a short code you can hand to someone else, who can play the very same valley.
